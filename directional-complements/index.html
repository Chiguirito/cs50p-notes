Part 1

The big idea: a direction becomes an idea

You already know these as movement words: 起来 = rise up, 下来 = come down, 出来 = come out. The HSK 3 leap is that Chinese reuses that same physical picture for abstract meanings. The trick to remembering them all is to see the metaphor — pick a complement below and watch its spatial meaning stretch.

Physical
站起来
→
Abstract
笑起来
起来 = rise up. A feeling or action can “rise up” into being too — that’s how 笑起来 means burst out laughing.

🧭 One mental rule for the whole page: keep the arrow in your head. 起来 points up (things begin / gather / surface), 下来 comes down and settles (stop, fix in place, last until now), 下去 keeps going onward, 出来 comes out into view (produce / detect), 过来 turns back to normal, 过去 passes away from normal.

Part 5

Where does the object go? The 起…来 split

When a verb + 起来 takes a concrete object, the object often splits the complement, sliding in between the two halves. No dragging — tap the object, then tap a gap.

Build: 拿 (pick up) + 起…来 + object 书 (book) = “pick up the book”.

Tap the object tile below, then tap a gap.

📌 With a short concrete object the split is the natural order: 拿起书来, 唱起歌来, 端起碗来. But for an abstract “recall” object, native speakers usually keep the complement whole and put the object after it — 想起来这件事 — or move it up front with 把: 把这件事想起来. A place word with 来/去 also comes before 来/去: 走下楼来.

Part 6

“Can” & “can’t”: the 得 / 不 infix

Slot 得 (can) or 不 (can’t) inside the complement and you get the super-common potential forms. 想起来 → 想得起来 (can recall) / 想不起来 (can’t recall). Flip the switch:

⚠️ The potential form only takes 得/不 in the middle — there is no 没-version. To say it didn’t happen, you negate the plain action instead (没想起来 = didn’t recall). And 得/不 go between the verb and the complement: 看得出来 / 看不出来, never ×不看出来.
